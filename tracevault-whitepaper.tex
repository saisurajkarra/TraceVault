% ===================== EXEC SUMMARY =====================
\section{Executive summary}

Every organization is losing its most valuable asset: the \emph{memory of how its work was done}.
Decisions, code, the reasoning behind them, who touched what, and --- increasingly --- the vast
output of AI assistants, all evaporate into scattered repos, local folders, chat logs, and
people's heads. The problem is accelerating: AI coding tools (Claude Code, Copilot, Cursor) now
generate enormous \emph{work exhaust} that no system captures.

\kw{tracevault} is a real data platform that ingests an organization's actual work --- a Git
repository, \emph{any folder} (most work is never committed), and real AI-assistant session logs ---
and turns it into a governed \kw{Apache Iceberg} lakehouse on object storage. It makes
\emph{every file ``speak''} (images captioned by a local vision model, documents extracted, code
embedded), derives a people\,$\leftrightarrow$\,files\,$\leftrightarrow$\,topics knowledge graph,
and surfaces the cross-silo ``\emph{has anyone already done this?}'' insight that no search box can.
It is queryable by two engines (\kw{Trino} distributed and \kw{DuckDB} embedded) over one shared
catalog, orchestrated with \kw{Dagster}, fed in real time by \kw{Kafka}, and observed with
\kw{Grafana}.

\callout{\textbf{This is not a deck.} The system described here is built and verified on real data:
\textbf{13{,}135 artifacts} and \textbf{9{,}167 edges} across four real projects (a 1{,}200-commit
Git repo and an 8{,}150-file non-git folder with its 26 real AI sessions and 946 file$\leftrightarrow$AI
links). The multi-engine lakehouse, the medallion marts, streaming, orchestration, and observability
all run today.}

\textbf{The billion-dollar thesis.} tracevault sits at the convergence of four large markets ---
enterprise search, knowledge management, developer-productivity tooling, and data catalog/governance
--- and creates a \emph{new} category: \kw{AI-era work governance}. Its moat is a data network effect:
the more of an organization's work it ingests, the more valuable cross-silo discovery becomes, and the
more it becomes the system of record that everything else plugs into. The open lakehouse earns trust
(you own your data); the derived intelligence layer (graph, marts, RAG) is the defensible product.

% ===================== PROBLEM =====================
\section{The problem: organizational amnesia in the AI era}

\subsection{Work is fragmented and most of it is invisible}
In a 100-person engineering org, knowledge lives in dozens of Git repos, but also in \emph{thousands
of files that never reach a commit}: local experiments, data, designs, scratch folders, documents
passed between teams. Traditional tools (code search, wikis, data catalogs) see only the committed,
structured slice. The rest --- often the most current work --- is dark.

\subsection{AI assistants created a new, ungoverned data exhaust}
Every prompt, every file an AI agent edited, every decision it reasoned through is now a first-class
record of how the work happened --- and it is being thrown away. As AI dev-tool adoption goes from
early to universal, this exhaust becomes the dominant record of engineering activity, and there is no
system of record for it. This is a greenfield category.

\subsection{The question nobody can answer}
``\emph{Has anyone here already solved this?}'' is the single most expensive question in a large org.
Answering it requires connecting people, files, commits, and AI sessions \emph{across silos} by
\emph{meaning}, not keywords. No existing product does this over the full breadth of real work.

% ===================== WHAT IT IS =====================
\section{What tracevault is}

tracevault ingests real work from three sources and produces five things on top of one lakehouse:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Ingestion.} A Git repo (\code{ingest\_git}), \emph{any folder} with no git
  (\code{ingest\_folder}), and real AI-assistant session logs (\code{ingest\_ai}). Live file changes
  stream in via Kafka (\code{streaming}).
  \item \textbf{``Every file speaks.''} \code{extract} translates any file into language: images are
  captioned by a local vision model (BLIP), PDF/DOCX/PPTX are extracted, code/text kept, binaries
  typed. Everything becomes addressable in one semantic space, connected to what people and the AI
  said about it.
  \item \textbf{A governed lakehouse.} Two Iceberg tables (\code{artifacts}, \code{edges}) physically
  on MinIO, partitioned by month, with ACID appends, snapshots, and time-travel.
  \item \textbf{Gold marts \& analytics.} Five medallion marts (author activity, file hotspots, topic
  summary, daily activity, and the headline \emph{cross-silo} pairs), queryable by DuckDB and Trino.
  \item \textbf{Discovery surfaces.} Semantic search, grounded RAG (Claude), an Obsidian-style
  knowledge graph, and a data-platform dashboard.
\end{itemize}

% ===================== ARCHITECTURE =====================
\section{Architecture: a medallion lakehouse}

tracevault follows the \kw{medallion} pattern (bronze\,$\rightarrow$\,silver\,$\rightarrow$\,gold) and
the open lakehouse architecture used by the most valuable data companies.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\bfseries}l X}
\toprule
\textcolor{tvamber}{Bronze} & Raw bytes in MinIO: content-addressed file/transcript blobs + the Iceberg warehouse. \\
\textcolor{tvmuted}{Silver} & Cleaned, typed Iceberg tables \code{artifacts} and \code{edges} --- the single source of truth. \\
\textcolor{tvamber}{Gold} & Five analytical marts, recomputed idempotently, queried via DuckDB \emph{and} Trino. \\
\textcolor{tvaccent}{Serve} & Search, RAG, knowledge graph, SQL console, time-travel, metrics --- over FastAPI. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The data model.} \code{artifacts}: \code{id, kind} (file\,$|$\,commit\,$|$\,ai\_session\,$|$\,ai\_message),
\code{source, repo, actor, created\_at} (month-partitioned), \code{title, text} (the searchable
language), \code{object\_uri} (MinIO blob), \code{extra}. \code{edges}: \code{src\_id, dst\_id, relation}
(authored\,$|$\,modified\,$|$\,co\_edited\,$|$\,ai\_touched\_file\,$|$\,message\_of\_session),
\code{weight}. Everything downstream --- embeddings, the graph, the marts --- derives from these two
tables.

\callout{\textbf{The multi-engine core (the hard part most demos fake).} pyiceberg (the application)
and \kw{Trino} (a real distributed SQL engine) read the \emph{exact same} Iceberg tables on MinIO
through \emph{one shared Postgres JDBC catalog}. The identical SQL query runs on DuckDB (embedded)
or Trino (distributed) from a single toggle --- proven live, not drawn.}

% ===================== MOAT =====================
\section{The moat: what nobody else has}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{It ingests the AI-era exhaust.} tracevault is, to our knowledge, the only system that
  ingests \emph{AI-assistant sessions} as first-class artifacts and links them to the real files they
  touched. As AI tools become the dominant author of engineering work, this becomes the system of
  record for ``what the AI did, and why.''
  \item \textbf{It captures uncommitted work.} Ingesting \emph{any folder}, not just git, closes the
  gap where most real work lives. Competitors see only the committed slice.
  \item \textbf{Multimodal ``speaking.''} Every file type --- images, PDFs, notebooks, binaries --- is
  translated into one language space and connected to what people and AI said about it. This is
  technically hard and unique.
  \item \textbf{Cross-silo discovery as a data product.} The \code{gold\_cross\_silo} mart surfaces
  ``who across teams has worked on related things'' --- e.g.\ two authors in different repositories
  sharing 17 topics. This is the defensible insight no search box produces.
  \item \textbf{A real open lakehouse, not an app.} Governance, lineage, ACID time-travel, and
  multi-engine SQL mean tracevault is infrastructure customers build on --- creating data gravity and
  switching costs.
\end{enumerate}

% ===================== BILLION DOLLAR =====================
\section{The case for a billion-dollar platform}

\subsection{Market: a convergence, plus a new category}
tracevault touches four established markets --- enterprise search, knowledge management,
developer-productivity tooling, and data catalog/governance --- each measured in billions. More
importantly, it \emph{defines} a new one: \kw{AI-era work governance}. Comparable companies validate
the value of each pillar: open lakehouse (Databricks, multi-tens-of-billions), enterprise
search/assistant (Glean, multi-billion), streaming (Confluent), analytics engineering (dbt Labs), data
catalog (Atlan/Collibra). tracevault is the intersection.

\subsection{Why now}
The trigger is AI adoption. Every team that adopts an AI coding assistant instantly creates a new,
valuable, ungoverned data stream --- and a new acute pain (``what did the AI change, who owns this,
has this been done?''). The category did not exist 24 months ago. First-mover advantage on the system
of record for AI-era work is the prize.

\subsection{The wedge and go-to-market}
Land in engineering organizations using AI dev tools, where the pain is sharpest and the value is
measurable (less duplicated work, faster onboarding, AI-change auditability). \textbf{Self-host first}
sells to security- and sovereignty-conscious enterprises that cannot send their code and AI logs to a
SaaS. Expand from engineering to the whole organization as more sources are ingested.

\subsection{The moat compounds (defensibility)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Data network effects.} Cross-silo discovery and topic graphs get more valuable with
  every additional source ingested --- value is super-linear in breadth.
  \item \textbf{Data gravity \& switching costs.} It becomes the org's lakehouse and memory; the
  derived graph/marts/RAG are sticky even though the raw data is open.
  \item \textbf{Open-but-owned.} The open lakehouse earns trust; the intelligence layer (graph,
  cross-silo, multimodal, RAG over AI exhaust) is the proprietary, hard-to-copy product.
  \item \textbf{Technical depth.} Multimodal + AI-trace ingestion + a verified multi-engine lakehouse
  is a real engineering moat, not a UI skin.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Business model}
Open-core: a self-hostable core (trust, adoption, sovereignty) plus an enterprise edition
(permissions, SSO, multi-tenant, managed cloud) on per-seat + usage pricing. Self-host keeps COGS on
the customer's infrastructure; the managed cloud captures lakehouse-compute margin. Land-and-expand as
ingested sources --- and thus value --- grow.

% ===================== ROADMAP / RISK =====================
\section{Roadmap and risks}

\subsection{Roadmap}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Permissions \& multi-tenancy} --- authentication, per-user authorization, and
  access-scoped search/graph/marts (the prerequisite for enterprise deployment).
  \item \textbf{Versioned catalog (Nessie/Polaris)} --- git-style branching of the lakehouse for safe
  experimentation and reproducibility.
  \item \textbf{Connectors} --- Slack, Jira, Google Drive, Notion, more AI assistants --- to widen the
  ingested surface and compound the network effect.
  \item \textbf{Managed cloud} --- the enterprise edition and SaaS control plane.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Risks and mitigations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Incumbent entry (e.g.\ search/code vendors).} Mitigation: open-lakehouse trust,
  AI-exhaust depth, and data gravity create a head start incumbents must rebuild against their SaaS
  models.
  \item \textbf{Data sensitivity.} Mitigation: self-hostable, runs in the customer's VPC, local models
  --- no data leaves the perimeter by default.
  \item \textbf{Scope creep.} Mitigation: the wedge (AI-using engineering orgs) is concrete and
  painful; expand only as the lakehouse proves value.
\end{itemize}
